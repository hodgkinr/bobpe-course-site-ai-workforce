AI Workforce Readiness: Practical Skills for Work

Know the input, own the output.

A six-week, asynchronous, tool-agnostic course for people preparing to enter the workforce. Learners build practical skills for defining tasks, providing context, iterating, evaluating outputs, protecting information, setting oversight, and designing an AI-supported workplace workflow. No programming prerequisite or paid AI account is required.

Learning objectives

  • Describe in plain language how generative AI produces outputs, distinguish generated responses from verified facts, and identify when information may be incomplete or stale.
  • Turn a workplace need into a clear request with goal, audience, context, constraints, and a useful output format; then improve the request through iteration.
  • Check claims and transformations against appropriate evidence, detect uncertainty or bias, and decide whether to accept, revise, verify further, or reject an output.
  • Choose what information is appropriate to share, account for privacy, fairness, accessibility, and policy, and retain human accountability.
  • Design a bounded professional workflow with appropriate AI or tool use, permissions, checkpoints, escalation paths, stop conditions, and evaluation.
  • Explain when AI is useful, when another method is preferable, and how to communicate the role and limits of AI-assisted work.
  • Explain a practical, plain-language model of how generative AI produces content from instructions and context.
  • Identify strengths, limits, uncertainty, and the need to verify generated material.
  • Choose a suitable low-risk AI use or explain why another method is preferable.
  • Convert a vague workplace request into a clear task brief.
  • Select relevant, approved context while protecting unnecessary or sensitive information.
  • Specify an output format that makes review and next steps practical.
  • Use targeted follow-up directions to improve a draft against explicit criteria.
  • Preserve accurate meaning, audience fit, and the writer’s intended voice.
  • Make an appropriate decision about communicating AI assistance.
  • Match important claims to suitable evidence and record the check.
  • Recognize missing information, uncertainty, and potential bias in an output.
  • Correct, qualify, escalate, or reject claims that the evidence does not support.
  • Apply data-minimization and approval checks before sharing information with an AI service.
  • Explain how an agent workflow can combine a model, instructions, tools, observations, and orchestration.
  • Match permissions, checkpoints, stop rules, and escalation to impact and reversibility.
  • Design a bounded, repeatable AI-supported workplace workflow with clear inputs, checks, and handoffs.
  • Evaluate a proposed workflow against a baseline and recommend adoption, revision, or non-use.
