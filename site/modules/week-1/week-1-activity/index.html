Make an AI Task-Fit Map

Learning objectives: Explain when AI is useful, when another method is preferable, and how to communicate the role and limits of AI-assisted work.; Choose a suitable low-risk AI use or explain why another method is preferable.

Content

Activity: Make an AI task-fit map

Objectives: explain-generative-ai, identify-ai-limits, choose-low-risk-fit Estimated time: 35 minutes Purpose: Practice choosing a method before opening a tool. You will classify fictional workplace tasks and explain what a human must check or decide.

Materials

Use the fictional scenarios below and the worksheet table. No AI account is needed. If you choose to compare a tool response, use only the invented details in this activity.

  1. A public library wants three possible headings for a fictional, public event flyer. The event name and date are invented.
  2. A fictional employer wants a chatbot to rank job applicants using resumes and select the top person.
  3. A community center has a made-up public checklist and wants it converted into a shorter checklist. A staff member can compare every item to the source.
  4. A worker wants a general explanation of the organization's current leave policy but has not provided an official policy document.

Steps

  1. For each scenario, write the task in one sentence beginning with an action verb. If it bundles more than one task, split it.
  2. Classify the proposed use as small reviewed trial, redesign with oversight, or use another method / do not use AI for this task.
  3. For each scenario, identify the proposed input and whether it is appropriate to share. If needed, propose a fictional or public substitute.
  4. Name the likely consequence of a wrong output and explain whether it is easy to check and undo.
  5. State the verification step and the human decision-maker. For tasks using another method, name the source or process you would use instead.
  6. Choose one scenario that seems suitable for a small trial. Write one success measure and one stop condition. Example: “Every date matches the event plan; stop if the draft adds facts not in the plan.”
  7. Finish with a two-sentence recommendation explaining why the tool is or is not a good fit for your selected task.

Deliverable

Submit a completed task-fit map for all four scenarios, plus the success measure, stop condition, and recommendation for one selected scenario. Aim for clear reasons rather than a particular label.

Success criteria

Your work is successful when it:

  • separates a bounded language or transformation task from a consequential decision;
  • identifies sensitive or missing input and does not recommend sharing private records with a generic service;
  • matches the review method to the output (source comparison for transformations, authoritative source for current policy, and qualified human process for hiring decisions);
  • names a human who retains accountability; and
  • gives a measurable success measure and a practical condition for stopping.

No-AI alternative

Complete the worksheet on paper. If you want a manual comparison, write a sample headline or shortened checklist yourself and compare it to the fictional scenario facts. The learning goal is the fit decision, not tool use.
