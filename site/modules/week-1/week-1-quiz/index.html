Week 1 Applied Judgment Quiz

Learning objectives: Describe in plain language how generative AI produces outputs, distinguish generated responses from verified facts, and identify when information may be incomplete or stale.; Explain when AI is useful, when another method is preferable, and how to communicate the role and limits of AI-assisted work.

Week 1: AI at work—useful, imperfect, optional

Scenario-based practice on generative AI capabilities, limits, and task fit.

Question 1. A coworker asks why a conversational AI can write a smooth paragraph but still give a wrong date. Which explanation is most useful?
A generative model produces likely language from learned patterns and conversation context; fluent wording does not verify factual details.
Question 2. A fictional arts center wants three headline ideas for a public open-house flyer. The worker will review them before publication. What is the best fit decision?
This is a bounded, low-consequence, reversible language task using public fictional information, and a person can review the result.
Question 3. A worker asks a generic chatbot to rank fictional job applicants from their resumes and select the person to hire. What should the worker do?
Hiring is consequential, involves personal information, and should follow approved procedures with accountable human decision-makers rather than delegated generic-model ranking.
Question 4. A staff member asks AI for the center's current refund policy, but does not provide an official policy source. What is the best next step?
Current policy should be checked in an authoritative source; a generated answer may be stale or unsupported.
Question 5. A worker wants AI to shorten a fictional public checklist. Which review best matches the task?
For a transformation, compare the shortened output against the source and confirm all required steps and conditions remain.
Question 6. A generated flyer draft says the open house requires registration, but the fictional event facts mention no registration. What should the worker do?
The registration requirement is unsupported. Remove it or verify it with an authoritative event source before using the draft.
Question 7. A worker can write a short fictional reminder in two minutes. Using AI would require prompting, checking, and correcting it. Which decision is most reasonable?
AI is optional; if the full assisted process adds effort without benefit, writing directly is a sound choice.
Question 8. A team has a public, fictional event plan and wants AI to draft an announcement. Which input practice is best?
Provide only relevant public facts and avoid unnecessary personal or confidential details.
Question 9. An AI response includes three citations supporting a workplace claim. What should the worker do before relying on the claim?
Generated citations may be inaccurate or irrelevant; open appropriate sources and verify that they directly support the claim.
Question 10. A worker is considering a small AI trial for a fictional checklist rewrite. Which plan best preserves human accountability?
A bounded trial defines safe input, review criteria, a human decision-maker, and a stop condition before use.
