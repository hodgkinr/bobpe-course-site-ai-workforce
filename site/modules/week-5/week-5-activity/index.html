Create an Oversight Plan for a Bounded Agent

Learning objectives: Choose what information is appropriate to share, account for privacy, fairness, accessibility, and policy, and retain human accountability.; Design a bounded professional workflow with appropriate AI or tool use, permissions, checkpoints, escalation paths, stop conditions, and evaluation.; Apply data-minimization and approval checks before sharing information with an AI service.; Match permissions, checkpoints, stop rules, and escalation to impact and reversibility.

Content

Activity: Create an Oversight Plan for a Bounded Agent

Scenario

A fictional office wants to use an AI-enabled assistant to help process supply requests. It may consult a fictional, approved catalog and prepare an unsubmitted order draft. It must not submit purchases, access payroll or personnel records, or make decisions about people. You are designing a plan before a pilot.

Steps

  1. State the goal and intended user in one sentence.
  2. List the minimum input needed. Use only fictional names, requests, and prices. Identify excluded information.
  3. Describe the system in plain language: model, instructions, tools, and any process manager/harness or orchestrator you assume. Focus on what it can access and do.
  4. Set the autonomy level: suggestion, draft, approved reversible action, or independent action. Explain why impact, reversibility, and uncertainty support that level.
  5. Specify at least two checkpoints, including exactly what the reviewer compares and which evidence source is used.
  6. Write at least three stop conditions and a human escalation path for each.
  7. Identify who owns the final decision and what should be recorded after a run.

You may complete this on paper. If using an AI service, use only the fictional scenario above; do not enter personal or employer information.

Deliverable

Submit a one-page oversight plan or a clearly labeled checklist containing: goal, allowed inputs, tool permissions, autonomy level and rationale, checkpoints, stop conditions, escalation, and human owner.

Success criteria

  • The workflow has a narrow, understandable purpose and uses minimum fictional or approved information.
  • Tool permissions match the stated task; draft authority does not silently include submission authority.
  • Oversight responds to impact, reversibility, and uncertainty.
  • Checkpoints name the evidence and the exact review action.
  • Stop conditions are observable and route exceptions to an appropriate person.
  • A named human retains responsibility for the final purchase decision.

No-AI alternative

Complete the plan from the scenario using the worksheet headings above. The learning objective is the quality of your boundary and oversight decisions, not access to a particular tool.
