Week 5 Applied Judgment Quiz

Learning objectives: Choose what information is appropriate to share, account for privacy, fairness, accessibility, and policy, and retain human accountability.; Design a bounded professional workflow with appropriate AI or tool use, permissions, checkpoints, escalation paths, stop conditions, and evaluation.; Apply data-minimization and approval checks before sharing information with an AI service.; Explain how an agent workflow can combine a model, instructions, tools, observations, and orchestration.; Match permissions, checkpoints, stop rules, and escalation to impact and reversibility.

Week 5: Privacy and Agent Oversight

Choose safe inputs and appropriate boundaries for connected AI workflows.

Question 1. A coordinator wants an AI tool to draft a general reminder about returning borrowed equipment. The coordinator has names, phone numbers, account notes, and a verified due-date policy. What is the safest useful input?
A reusable reminder needs the general purpose, tone, and relevant policy wording, not identifiable account records. A human can verify the due date in the approved system before sending.
Question 2. A learner removes a person’s name but keeps a rare job title, small site location, exact incident date, and unusual event. What should they do before using this as a practice example?
Details can identify a person in combination. Generalize or replace the scenario with invented details rather than assuming that removing a name is enough.
Question 3. A staff member wants a general-purpose AI service to summarize a client file containing health and financial details. They have not checked policy. What is the best next step?
Sensitive information should not be entered until the person confirms the service and use are approved. They should ask an authorized privacy or policy contact and consider a safer alternative.
Question 4. A training coordinator asks an AI system to identify which employees are ‘most committed’ based on uneven coaching notes. What concern should guide the response?
The input may be incomplete or uneven, and ‘commitment’ may not be a fair or relevant criterion. A model-generated ranking can make those issues appear neutral; use consistent approved criteria and human decision-making.
Question 5. A supply assistant only needs to prepare a proposed order from an approved catalog. Which permission boundary best matches the task?
Read-only catalog access and the ability to prepare an unsubmitted draft fit the purpose. Submission or payment authority is broader than needed and should remain with a person.
Question 6. An agent can create a draft supply order, but submitting it spends money and is difficult to reverse. What autonomy choice is most appropriate for an initial pilot?
Because submission has financial impact and is hard to reverse, keep the agent at draft level and require an authorized human to verify and submit.
Question 7. Which instruction gives a reviewer the clearest checkpoint before an order is submitted?
A useful checkpoint states the fields to compare and the evidence source before the consequential action.
Question 8. An agent cannot retrieve the current catalog price and has only an old cached amount. What should the workflow do?
The missing current evidence is a stop condition. The agent should not infer that an old price is still valid; it should route the case for a current lookup or manual handling.
Question 9. A workflow retrieves a schedule, summarizes it, then drafts a message. The retrieved page is unavailable, but the system continues with a generated date. What design improvement is most useful?
The workflow should treat source failure as a stop condition and prevent downstream steps from turning an unsupported guess into a message. A human can locate a current source or use the manual process.
Question 10. A pilot has permissions, checkpoints, and a stop rule, but no one is responsible for reviewing errors or updating the source. What should be added?
A responsible human owner must maintain the source, review problems, and have authority to pause the workflow. Controls need ownership to remain effective over time.
