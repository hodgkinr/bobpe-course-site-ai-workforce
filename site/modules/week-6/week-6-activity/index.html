Build a Workflow Dossier

Learning objectives: Choose what information is appropriate to share, account for privacy, fairness, accessibility, and policy, and retain human accountability.; Design a bounded professional workflow with appropriate AI or tool use, permissions, checkpoints, escalation paths, stop conditions, and evaluation.; Apply data-minimization and approval checks before sharing information with an AI service.; Match permissions, checkpoints, stop rules, and escalation to impact and reversibility.; Design a bounded, repeatable AI-supported workplace workflow with clear inputs, checks, and handoffs.; Evaluate a proposed workflow against a baseline and recommend adoption, revision, or non-use.

Content

Activity: Build and Evaluate a Workplace Workflow

Objectives: design-ai-workflow; evaluate-ai-workflow; protect-sensitive-information; set-oversight.

Purpose

Create a small, transferable dossier for a repeatable, low-risk task from a target role or a fictional workplace. This activity scaffolds the final project. Choose drafting, summarizing, sorting, or formatting; do not start with a high-stakes decision about a person.

Steps

  1. Define the task, intended users, affected people, and current baseline. Identify what makes the task useful to improve.
  2. Map inputs and handling rules. Use fictional or public information; state what must never be entered and whether a service must be approved.
  3. Write the step sequence and identify exactly one AI contribution. State what tools may read or change and what actions remain prohibited.
  4. Add checkpoints, stop conditions, escalation, and a human owner. Tie checks to evidence and define a responsible handoff.
  5. Create at least two fictional cases, including one ordinary case and one boundary or failure case. Compare the baseline and AI-supported process. If using a tool, do not enter personal or employer-confidential data. A paper simulation is acceptable.
  6. Record quality, total time (including review), accessibility, errors, corrections, and escalations. Mark estimates clearly and avoid broad claims from a small sample.
  7. Recommend adopt, revise, or avoid. State the evidence behind the recommendation and limitations of the test.

Deliverable

Submit a workflow dossier of approximately 2–4 pages or an equivalent structured worksheet. Include: task and baseline; intended users and affected people; safe inputs; workflow steps and tool boundary; sample prompt/context or paper instructions; verification and review checkpoints; stop/escalation rules; at least two test cases with evidence; time/quality/accessibility trade-offs; and an adopt/revise/avoid recommendation.

Success criteria

  • The task is specific, repeatable, and suitable for a bounded experiment.
  • Inputs are minimized and privacy limits are explicit.
  • The workflow shows where AI acts, what it cannot do, and who is accountable.
  • Evidence-based review occurs before consequential or external action.
  • Failure cases trigger a clear stop or escalation rather than a guess.
  • The comparison includes the baseline and counts human review/correction effort.
  • The recommendation is proportionate to the evidence and names limitations.

No-AI alternative

Run both paths as a paper simulation. Use the same fictional cases and reference material, write the AI step as a proposed response, and have a peer or your own checklist perform the review. The evaluation and design decisions remain the same.
