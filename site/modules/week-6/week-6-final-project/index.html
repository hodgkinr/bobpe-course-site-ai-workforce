Responsible AI Workflow Dossier: Final Project

Learning objectives: Describe in plain language how generative AI produces outputs, distinguish generated responses from verified facts, and identify when information may be incomplete or stale.; Turn a workplace need into a clear request with goal, audience, context, constraints, and a useful output format; then improve the request through iteration.; Check claims and transformations against appropriate evidence, detect uncertainty or bias, and decide whether to accept, revise, verify further, or reject an output.; Choose what information is appropriate to share, account for privacy, fairness, accessibility, and policy, and retain human accountability.; Design a bounded professional workflow with appropriate AI or tool use, permissions, checkpoints, escalation paths, stop conditions, and evaluation.; Explain when AI is useful, when another method is preferable, and how to communicate the role and limits of AI-assisted work.; Design a bounded, repeatable AI-supported workplace workflow with clear inputs, checks, and handoffs.; Evaluate a proposed workflow against a baseline and recommend adoption, revision, or non-use.

Content

Final Project: Responsible AI Workflow Dossier

Objectives: design-ai-workflow; evaluate-ai-workflow; protect-sensitive-information; set-oversight.

Purpose

Design and evaluate a bounded AI-supported workflow for a repeatable task in a target role or a fictional workplace. The project brings together the course principle Know the input, own the output. You are responsible for the information boundary, the checks, the handoff, and the recommendation. A well-supported decision to use a non-AI method is acceptable when the task or evidence does not support AI use.

Figure: Know the input, check the draft, and own the delivered work.

Choose a low-risk task such as preparing a reusable draft, sorting routine requests, summarizing public material, or formatting a report. Do not use real personal, employer-confidential, client, student, or regulated information in a general AI service. Use fictional or public information, or follow an explicitly approved organizational process. Do not choose a workflow that makes an unreviewed decision about an individual’s employment, education, health, finances, access, or safety.

Required dossier sections

  1. Task and baseline: Describe the task, its purpose, the current steps, who performs them, and the baseline time or quality evidence. Label estimates as estimates.
  2. Users and affected people: Identify who uses the output and who could be affected by it. Describe relevant needs, including accessibility.
  3. Inputs and data rules: List the minimum information required, its source and sensitivity, approved handling, exclusions, and safer alternatives.
  4. Workflow and tool boundary: Show the steps from request to handoff. Identify the AI contribution and specify what each tool may read, create, change, send, or not do.
  5. Sample request and context: Include a representative prompt or paper instruction using only fictional/public information. Include goal, context, constraints, audience, and output format as relevant.
  6. Verification and human review: State what evidence the reviewer checks, how the review happens, who owns the decision, and where review occurs before a consequential action.
  7. Failure, stop, and escalation rules: Include observable stop conditions and what happens next for missing evidence, ambiguity, tool failure, sensitive cases, or out-of-scope requests.
  8. Test evidence: Compare the proposed process with the baseline using at least two cases. Include at least one ordinary case and one boundary, uncertainty, or failure case. Show outputs or paper-simulation results, corrections, sources checked, and total time including human review.
  9. Trade-offs and limitations: Discuss time, quality, accessibility, consistency, privacy, review burden, and what the test does not establish.
  10. Recommendation and handoff: Recommend adopt, revise and retest, or avoid/non-use. Explain the evidence behind the decision and tell a future operator what the workflow does, what it cannot establish, and who maintains or can pause it.

Submission alternatives

Submit a written dossier (recommended length: 3–6 pages), a slide deck with speaker notes, or an accessible narrated presentation with a separate evidence table. The format may vary, but all ten sections and the evidence must be present and readable. A paper-based simulation is acceptable; no paid account or specific AI product is required. If you use a tool, disclose which part it assisted with and independently verify factual claims.

Evaluation rubric

| Criterion | 4 — Strong and transferable | 3 — Meets expectations | 2 — Developing | 1 — Needs substantial revision | |---|---|---|---|---| | Task fit and baseline | Defines a narrow, repeatable task; baseline is clear and supported or transparently estimated; affected people and purpose are explicit. | Task and baseline are understandable, with minor gaps in scope or evidence. | Task is broad or baseline is incomplete; affected people or purpose are only partly clear. | Task is undefined, unsuitable, or lacks a baseline and affected-person consideration. | | Inputs and responsibility | Minimizes information, states sensitivity and exclusions, uses safe examples, and assigns human ownership clearly. | Inputs are mostly appropriate and a human owner is identified; one handling detail needs clarification. | Includes unnecessary or ambiguous inputs, or responsibility is not fully assigned. | Uses unsafe information or implies that the AI owns a consequential decision. | | Workflow boundary and oversight | Steps, permissions, autonomy, evidence checkpoints, stop rules, and escalation are specific and proportionate to impact and reversibility. | Workflow and review are usable, with minor ambiguity in a permission, checkpoint, or exception path. | Some controls exist but key permissions, checks, or failure paths are vague. | Workflow has no effective boundary or permits unsupported/high-impact action without review. | | Verification and evidence | At least two relevant cases include ordinary and boundary behavior; claims are checked against appropriate sources; total human effort and corrections are visible. | Cases and checks are present, with a small gap in comparison or evidence detail. | Testing is narrow, omits review effort, or does not clearly connect claims to sources. | Evidence is absent, unsafe, or used to make claims it cannot support. | | Accessibility, trade-offs, and recommendation | Evaluates accessibility and meaningful costs; recommendation is proportionate, acknowledges limits, and gives a clear handoff. | Recommendation is supported and limitations are stated; one trade-off or handoff item needs more detail. | Recommendation or accessibility discussion is thin or exceeds what the evidence supports. | Recommendation ignores material risks, affected people, limitations, or next steps. | | Communication | Dossier is organized, plain-language, accessible, and understandable to a new operator. | Mostly clear, with minor organization or terminology issues. | Important sections are difficult to follow or use unexplained terms. | A reader cannot reliably understand or operate the proposed workflow. |

Self-check before submission

  • [ ] My task is specific, repeatable, and appropriate for a bounded test.
  • [ ] I used fictional/public information or documented an approved process; no prohibited details are present.
  • [ ] I named affected people, considered accessibility, and identified the human decision owner.
  • [ ] I distinguished what the AI may suggest or draft from what a person may approve, send, or decide.
  • [ ] My checkpoints point to evidence and happen before consequential actions.
  • [ ] My stop conditions cover missing/unclear input, unavailable sources, tool failures, and out-of-scope cases.
  • [ ] I compared the baseline and proposed workflow on at least two cases and counted review/correction effort.
  • [ ] I marked estimates and limitations and made a recommendation proportionate to the evidence.
  • [ ] A new operator can tell what to do, when to pause, and who maintains the workflow.
