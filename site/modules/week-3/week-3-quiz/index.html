Week 3 Applied Judgment Quiz

Learning objectives: Use targeted follow-up directions to improve a draft against explicit criteria.; Preserve accurate meaning, audience fit, and the writer’s intended voice.; Make an appropriate decision about communicating AI assistance.

Week 3: Iteration, voice, and communication

Scenario-based practice in steering drafts, preserving meaning, and deciding about transparency.

Question 1. A fictional center’s notice says the front desk opens at 10 a.m., the building opens at 8:30 a.m., and programs are unchanged. A draft says all activities move to 10 a.m. What is the best first step before requesting a revision?
Name the specific factual mismatch and the intended information before directing a revision.
Question 2. A draft buries the date in its final sentence. Which follow-up is most useful?
The best direction describes a visible change tied to the reader’s need.
Question 3. After two revisions, a short notice meets every criterion and matches its approved source. What is the strongest next choice?
A stopping rule prevents unnecessary rounds once the work meets the defined need.
Question 4. A generated reminder says a fictional office is closed “for staff training,” but the supplied schedule gives no reason. What should the learner do?
Remove the unsupported cause or verify it with an authorized source before including it.
Question 5. A notice has a friendly tone but lists the wrong opening time. Which revision sequence is best?
Correct factual defects against the source before deciding whether style changes are needed.
Question 6. A job-seeker’s draft says they are “an ideal fit” and have “extensive experience,” but their notes only show interest in an entry-level role. What is the best edit?
Keep claims grounded in the writer’s notes and replace inflated statements with accurate intent.
Question 7. A learner says a generated email “doesn’t sound like me.” Which direction is most actionable?
Observable qualities such as sentence length and tone help guide a revision better than a vague request.
Question 8. A revised thank-you note is concise, but includes a promise the learner never intended to make. What should happen before sending?
The writer must remove or confirm every commitment and stand behind the final message.
Question 9. A workplace rule says employees must disclose AI-generated text in public notices. A learner used an AI tool to draft a notice. What should they do?
Follow the applicable policy and use its requested disclosure approach.
Question 10. A learner used an AI tool only to shorten a routine fictional reminder, checked every fact, and no policy or audience expectation requires disclosure. Which conclusion is most reasonable?
Disclosure decisions depend on applicable rules, the contribution, audience expectations, and accurate description; there is no universal announcement requirement.
