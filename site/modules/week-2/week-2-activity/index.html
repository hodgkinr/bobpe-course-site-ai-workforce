Build and Review a Task Brief

Learning objectives: Turn a workplace need into a clear request with goal, audience, context, constraints, and a useful output format; then improve the request through iteration.; Choose what information is appropriate to share, account for privacy, fairness, accessibility, and policy, and retain human accountability.; Convert a vague workplace request into a clear task brief.; Select relevant, approved context while protecting unnecessary or sensitive information.

Content

Activity: Build and review a task brief

Objectives: convert-vague-request, provide-safe-context, specify-output-format Estimated time: 40 minutes Purpose: Turn a vague request into a safe, specific, checkable task brief and practice evaluating the output.

Fictional case

A fictional community center is preparing a reminder for first-time visitors to its open house. The invented facts are: the event is free, starts Thursday at 6 p.m., visitors may tour the art rooms, and the west entrance is open. A draft request says only: “Make this better for people.”

Steps

  1. Rewrite the request as a task brief with five labeled parts: goal, audience, context, constraints, and output shape.
  2. Keep the input limited to the four fictional facts above. Add at least two constraints that can be checked. Do not invent a registration requirement, address, contact, or other fact.
  3. Specify an output format that will make it easy to review. For instance, request one paragraph and a separate list of facts used.
  4. Decide how you will review the result before generating anything. Write three required facts and one kind of unsupported addition to watch for.
  5. Optional tool practice: submit your brief to a no-cost conversational AI service. Use only the fictional case information. Review the output against your criteria and mark each sentence keep, revise, or remove. If the service asks you for personal or workplace details, do not provide them.
  6. If the first result misses a constraint, write one targeted follow-up that names the gap and restates the boundary. Review any revision using the same criteria.
  7. Record one human decision you made and whether the tool contributed enough value to continue. You can decide that drafting it yourself was more effective.

Deliverable

Submit the completed five-part brief, review criteria, and a short review record. If you use an AI tool, include the fictional output and mark what you kept, revised, or removed. If you use the no-AI path, provide your own draft and review it against the same brief.

Success criteria

Your artifact:

  • states an observable goal and identifies the first-time visitor audience;
  • includes only the relevant invented facts and no sensitive real-world information;
  • provides at least two checkable constraints and a usable output format;
  • includes a review method tied to the source facts;
  • identifies an unsupported detail to reject; and
  • records a human decision about what to use and why.

No-AI alternative

Write the reminder yourself using the fictional facts, then use your task brief and criteria to inspect your draft. You can also ask a peer to review whether the requested format and constraints are clear. Do not introduce real visitor information.
