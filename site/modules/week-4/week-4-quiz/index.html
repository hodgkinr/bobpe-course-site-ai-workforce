Week 4 Applied Judgment Quiz

Learning objectives: Match important claims to suitable evidence and record the check.; Recognize missing information, uncertainty, and potential bias in an output.; Correct, qualify, escalate, or reject claims that the evidence does not support.

Week 4: Verify, interpret, and take responsibility

Scenario-based practice in matching claims to evidence, recognizing uncertainty, and correcting unsupported statements.

Question 1. A generated staff update says a fictional office follows a 48-hour closure-notice rule. Which source is most appropriate to verify the claim?
A current, authoritative policy is the proper evidence for a policy requirement.
Question 2. An AI summary says a schedule confirms that Room B closes June 10. What is the best way to verify before publishing?
Inspect the schedule itself and confirm it supports the exact room and date claim.
Question 3. A fictional report says 18 of 20 affected visitors received a text. The available note lists 18 delivered messages and 20 affected visitors but gives no matching definitions or dates. What is the best response?
The claim requires aligned groups, periods, and definitions; the counts alone do not establish the ratio.
Question 4. A schedule confirms a room is unavailable on Wednesday, but no source explains why. A draft says an inspection found a fault. What should the reviewer do?
Retain the verified schedule detail and remove or verify the unsupported explanation.
Question 5. An attendance table records fewer visits from Group B during four workshops. A generated draft calls Group B “less committed.” Which interpretation is best supported?
The table supports a limited statement about recorded visits, not motivation or commitment.
Question 6. A draft says “Everyone prefers morning meetings” based on feedback from three fictional coworkers. What is the best revision?
The wording should reflect the limited sample rather than generalize to everyone.
Question 7. A fictional table compares attendance between two groups but does not show invitation totals or access barriers. Which reviewer question is most useful?
Comparability and missing context can affect how a group comparison should be interpreted.
Question 8. A draft includes an important safety instruction, but the current procedure is not available and the wording could affect visitor behavior. What should the learner do?
High-impact instructions require an authoritative check; pause use and seek the responsible source or reviewer.
Question 9. A reviewer removes an unsupported statement about visitor motivation after checking the attendance table. What should the correction record include?
A useful record names the claim, evidence, change, and unresolved follow-up so others can understand the decision.
Question 10. A generated draft includes a citation that appears to support a policy claim. What is the best next step?
Treat the citation as a lead, then inspect the actual current source and confirm it supports the exact claim.
