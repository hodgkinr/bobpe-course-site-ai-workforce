Audit a Flawed Workplace Draft

Learning objectives: Check claims and transformations against appropriate evidence, detect uncertainty or bias, and decide whether to accept, revise, verify further, or reject an output.; Match important claims to suitable evidence and record the check.; Recognize missing information, uncertainty, and potential bias in an output.

Content

Activity: Audit a flawed workplace draft

Aligned objectives: verify-ai-claims, assess-uncertainty, correct-unsupported-claims Estimated time: 35–45 minutes

Scenario

A fictional coordinator at Harbor Street Community Center asks a conversational AI tool to summarize a supplied sample schedule and attendance table. The tool returns this draft:

> “Room B will close Wednesday, June 10, because an inspection found an electrical fault. Policy requires 48 hours’ notice, so all visitors must be texted by Monday. Last month, 18 of 20 visitors received a text. Group B is less committed to workshops because it attended fewer sessions, so the center should reduce invitations to that group.”

The supplied sample schedule says only that Room B is unavailable June 10. The sample attendance table records 12 visits by Group A and 6 visits by Group B across four evening workshops. It does not identify unique participants, invitations, reasons for attendance, or whether messages were delivered. No inspection report, notification log, or current policy is included. All scenario details are fictional.

Steps

  1. Break the draft into claims. Underline every date, cause, policy, number, description of people, and recommendation.
  2. Create a claim log. Use four columns: claim; evidence needed; result/status; action. Possible actions include retain, correct, qualify, verify further, remove, or escalate.
  3. Match evidence to each claim. Identify the appropriate source for each one. Do not treat the generated draft as its own evidence.
  4. Assess uncertainty and framing. Mark missing information and any statement that goes beyond the measure. Ask whether the comparison is fair and whether labels assign motives not recorded in the data.
  5. Rewrite the update. Use only what the supplied fictional materials support. State a necessary limitation or follow-up question. Do not invent an inspection cause, policy, or message count.
  6. Write a correction record. Explain what you changed, what you checked, what could not be checked, and what a responsible person should do next.

Deliverable

Submit one claim log, a corrected draft of 80–150 words, and a correction record of 3–6 sentences. Use fictional information only; do not paste real workplace or personal records into an AI service.

Success criteria

  • The log separates all major claims and identifies evidence that could verify each one.
  • Generated wording is not treated as a source.
  • Unsupported cause, policy, notification count, and motivation claim are removed or clearly marked for verification.
  • The rewrite reports the supplied schedule and attendance evidence without implying more than it shows.
  • The correction record distinguishes what was checked from what remains unknown and names a sensible next step.
  • The learner uses neutral, accessible language and keeps human responsibility visible.

No-AI alternative

Complete the audit with the scenario text and a paper or word-processing document. The activity assesses claim analysis and judgment, not use of a particular service.
